\begin{abstract}
Does a bounded draft before an action improve an agent's decisions? Two completed
1,776-episode studies compared Direct and Draft procedures in NVIDIA Nemotron
Nano and Super on Nebius Token Factory, first at temperature 1 and then at 0.
Drafting lowered average exact benchmark loss for both models at both temperatures,
but worsened performance against Tit-for-Tat on average. Super's drafting advantage
was smaller at zero: its Direct result improved while its Draft result was nearly
unchanged. Autonomous mutual cooperation occurred in 9.2\% of rounds at temperature
1 and 11.8\% at 0. These are descriptive amended studies, with matched environment
schedules but different execution windows. They do not isolate a causal temperature
effect or establish a general model ranking.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and contribution}
Cooperation counts alone do not establish decision quality: an agent can earn
more by cooperating less. Dilemma Dynamics separates exact own-payoff loss against
committed policies from interaction between adaptive agents. For students examining
incentives and builders inspecting agents, it offers an action-by-action replay,
a public dataset of 3,552 completed episodes and offline score reproduction.
Educational outcomes and real-world negotiation performance have not been evaluated.

\section{Design}
We compared Nano (30B-A3B) and Super (120B-A12B). Hosted IDs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B}
\item \texttt{nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b}
\end{itemize}
Players maximised expected own cumulative points in a repeated Prisoner's Dilemma:
\(CC=(3,3)\), \(CD=(0,5)\), \(DC=(5,0)\), \(DD=(1,1)\).
Choices stayed private until both players acted.

Direct used one thinking-off action request (128-token cap). Draft used a
thinking-on draft (1,024-token cap), then a separate thinking-off action request
(128-token cap). Both received full completed history; only the current draft
was added to the action request. Each completed study used its selected temperature
for every phase, with top-\(p=1\). Drafting changes compute, request count, thinking
settings and conditioning together; it is not an equal-cost intervention.

Each temperature completed the same 24-block schedule: 1,536 Track A episodes
against disclosed AllC, AllD, Tit-for-Tat (TFT) and Grim policies, plus 240 Track B
autonomous dyads spanning all ten unordered model--procedure pairings, including
self-play. Track A has 16 conditions: each policy at \(\delta=.4,.6,.9\), one-round
AllC/AllD, and ten-round TFT/Grim. Track B uses \(\delta=.9\).
Four label/seat strata have six blocks each. Episode order, IDs, environment seeds,
stopping lengths and presentations match across temperatures. Provider sampling
randomness was not matched. A geometric game's final round was hidden when choosing.

\section{Scoring and primary results}
At state \(s_t\), the gap for action \(a_t\) is
\(g_t=V^*(s_t)-Q^*(s_t,a_t)\), with optimal continuation after that action.
Episode loss is
\[
L_{\rm geometric}=(1-\delta)\sum_t g_t,
\qquad L_{\rm fixed}=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{t=1}^{H}g_t.
\]
Geometric gaps are not discounted again: continuation is in \(Q^*\), and
termination supplies survival weighting. Lower loss means fewer achievable own
points given up, not more cooperation or a percentage of correct actions.
For example, in a one-round game against AllC, C earns 3 and D earns 5: C has
loss 2 and D has loss 0.

The primary contrast is Draft minus Direct mean loss within each model and
temperature, equally weighting 16 conditions and four strata. Each procedure has
384 benchmark episodes per temperature. Approximate 95\% intervals use 20,000
stratified whole-block bootstrap draws, preserving related observations together.
There are only six blocks per stratum; interval coverage and power are not established.

\section{Matched temperature sensitivity}
The temperature-zero extension was requested after reviewing temperature-one
results. We pair corresponding blocks across the two studies rather than pool
48 blocks as independent repetitions. For each arm, compute its block mean loss
at 0 minus its mean at 1; for the interaction, subtract the corresponding
Direct change from the Draft change. Resample the same six matched blocks within
each of four strata, using 20,000 draws. Positive interaction means a smaller
Draft advantage at zero. Intervals are approximate, with no multiplicity correction.

\end{figure}

Super Direct improved substantially at zero while Super Draft was nearly
unchanged, narrowing Super's Draft advantage. Nano Draft had higher mean loss
at zero; the interval for Nano's interaction spans zero. These comparisons do
not support a universal preferred temperature. Different dates, provider/backend
windows and recovery policies also differed; temperature attribution is confounded.
Temperature zero does not guarantee deterministic hosted inference.

\section{Interpretation}
A best response to a commitment differs from an equilibrium \cite{rasmusen}.
Grim's initial cooperation threshold is \(\delta=1/2\); TFT's is \(\delta=2/3\).
With ten announced rounds, either committed policy is best answered by nine
cooperations then defection (32 own points). Finite-game backward induction for
two strategic players does not imply immediate defection against these commitments.

\section{Amendments and limitations}
The temperature-one study followed an eight-block local attempt stopped at
339/592 episodes by HTTP 502. Its outcomes had been inspected before the fresh
24-block successor. The first temperature-zero attempt stopped at 62/1,776
completed episodes on HTTP 529, which its frozen recovery policy did not permit
retrying. The separately identified temperature-zero recovery executed all 24
blocks with a prospective policy admitting bounded retries for 529. Neither
completed run actually needed a retry. Interrupted and engineering observations
are excluded from the public scientific datasets; original evidence and guards
remain retained privately. These are descriptive amendments, not fresh
confirmatory preregistrations.

One environment schedule, four policies, fixed payoffs and one provider window
per temperature limit generalisation. Provider IDs do not authenticate immutable
weight versions. Draft text is not verified internal belief. Extra requests,
compute, thinking settings and draft conditioning change together. The temperature
comparison followed earlier outcome inspection and is confounded by execution
windows and recovery policies. No universal reasoning superiority, causal
temperature effect or educational benefit is established.

\section{Execution and costs}
Each completed final run recorded 17,124 successful requests, zero retries,
zero protocol failures and no unresolved reservations. Approximate final durations
were 3h08m at temperature 1 and 2h15m at 0, measured from the session start to the
terminal ledger's filesystem modification (the terminal event has no timestamp).
Usage-priced final inference estimates were \$4.92776508 and \$4.47913740,
respectively: \$9.40690248 combined, excluding engineering and failed predecessors.
These are estimates at recorded 20 October rates, not provider invoices.

\section{Related work}
Akata et al.\ \cite{akata} study LLM behaviour in repeated games and a social
chain-of-thought intervention. Fontana et al.\ \cite{fontana} examine cooperative
behaviour against adversaries of varying hostility. Pal et al.\ \cite{pal}
examine strategies, continuation probabilities, horizons and framing in repeated
Prisoner's Dilemma. Our narrower contribution compares bounded Direct/Draft
procedures in two hosted Nemotron deployments using exact committed-opponent
loss and a matched temperature sensitivity analysis, alongside autonomous pairing
outcomes. It does not establish superiority over those studies' models, tasks or methods.

\section{Reproduction and public evidence}
Repository: \href{https://github.com/sheinkmana/dilemma-dynamics}{github.com/sheinkmana/dilemma-dynamics}.
The public app offers every released episode at either temperature and aggregate
comparisons. Each temperature has a separate executed plan and source index.
Episode IDs recur across matched studies; identify an observation by temperature
and episode ID together. Original temperature-one data remain at the package
root; temperature-zero data are additive under \path{public/research/temperature-zero/}.

From the repository root, with Python 3.12 and the frozen environment:
\begin{verbatim}
uv sync --frozen
uv run python tools/verify_public_results.py
\end{verbatim}
The verifier recomputes all 3,552 episode scores, four primary bootstrap
intervals, six matched temperature intervals and nine CSVs, checks the public
checksum manifest, and verifies cost arithmetic against dated rates. Scoring,
analysis, design and dependency versions are checked against each study's
executed source index; the original design is retained byte for byte as
\texttt{design\_v1.py}. App and report revisions do not replace frozen execution evidence.
See \path{public/research/README.md} for the file map and exact bootstrap seed convention.

The released plans and actions are unchanged copies of verified exports. Raw
provider responses, private drafts, receipts, executed source archives and
operational records remain private. Public data reproduce scores but cannot
independently authenticate provider identity, transport history, raw event chains
or billing. Checksums detect changes; they are not signatures. Reference works
are cited and are not redistributed.
