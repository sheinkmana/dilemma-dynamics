\begin{abstract}
Does a bounded draft before an action improve an agent's decisions? In a
completed 1,776-episode study using two NVIDIA Nemotron models on Nebius Token
Factory, drafting lowered average exact benchmark loss, but worsened performance
against Tit-for-Tat on average. Autonomous cooperation varied by pairing.
Dilemma Dynamics combines inspectable play with a public action dataset and offline
score reproduction. This amended study is descriptive, not a general model ranking.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and contribution}

For students examining incentives and builders inspecting agents, cooperation
counts alone do not establish decision quality. A model can earn more by
cooperating less. We separate exact own-payoff loss against committed policies
from outcomes between adaptive agents. The contribution is this inspectable
Nemotron procedure comparison and its reproducible action dataset, not a first
demonstration of LLM cooperation. Educational impact and real-world negotiation
performance have not been evaluated.

\section{Design}

We compared direct action with a bounded draft-then-action procedure in two
Nemotron models: Nano (30B-A3B) and Super (120B-A12B).\footnote{Hosted IDs:
\texttt{nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B} and
\texttt{nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b}.}
Players maximised expected own cumulative points in a repeated Prisoner's
Dilemma: \(CC=(3,3)\), \(CD=(0,5)\), \(DC=(5,0)\), \(DD=(1,1)\).
Choices were private until both players had acted.

Direct used one thinking-off action request (128-token cap). Draft used a
thinking-on draft (1,024-token cap), then a separate thinking-off action
request (128-token cap). Both received full completed history and used
temperature 1 and top-\(p=1\). Only the current draft was added to the action request.

All 1,776 scheduled episodes completed in 24 balanced blocks:
\begin{itemize}
\item Track A: 1,536 episodes against disclosed AllC, AllD, Tit-for-Tat (TFT)
or Grim policies. The 16 conditions covered continuation probabilities
\(\delta=0.4,0.6,0.9\), one-round AllC/AllD, and ten-round TFT/Grim.
\item Track B: 240 autonomous dyads covering all ten unordered pairings of
the four model--procedure configurations, including self-play, at \(\delta=0.9\).
\end{itemize}
Four label/seat strata had six blocks each. Arms shared environment draws and
stopping lengths within blocks; provider sampling randomness was not matched.

\section{Scoring}

Track A loss measures expected payoff forgone against a committed opponent.
The gap for action \(a_t\) at state \(s_t\) is
\(g_t=V^*(s_t)-Q^*(s_t,a_t)\), where continuation after that action is optimal.
Episode loss is
\[
L_{\mathrm{geometric}}=(1-\delta)\sum_t g_t,
\qquad
L_{\mathrm{fixed}}=\frac{1}{H}\sum_{t=1}^{H}g_t.
\]
Geometric gaps are not discounted again: continuation is in \(Q^*\), and
termination supplies survival weighting. Lower loss is better; it measures
neither action accuracy nor cooperation.

The primary contrast is draft minus direct mean loss within each model.
The 16 conditions and four strata receive equal weight. Approximate 95\%
intervals use 20,000 stratified whole-block bootstrap draws.

\paragraph{Worked example.}
Against Always Cooperate in a one-round game, choosing C earns 3 points whereas
D earns 5. The loss of C is therefore $5-3=2$; the loss of D is zero. This is
foregone own payoff, not a moral judgement or cooperation score. A block repeats
all conditions; a presentation stratum groups an action mapping and a player seat.

\section{Interpretation and limitations}

A best response to a commitment differs from an equilibrium \cite{rasmusen}.
Grim's initial cooperation threshold is \(\delta=1/2\); TFT's is \(\delta=2/3\).
With ten announced rounds, either committed policy is best answered by nine
cooperations then defection (32 own points). Finite-game backward induction
for two strategic players does not imply immediate defection against these
commitments. Geometric termination was hidden when choosing.

An earlier run stopped at 339/592 episodes after HTTP 502. Its outcomes were
inspected before this successor, which retained the original 24-block schedule
and excluded predecessor and engineering observations. These results are
descriptive, not a new confirmatory preregistration.

Drafting changes compute, request count, thinking settings and conditioning
together. Six blocks per stratum, one schedule, four policies and one provider
window limit generalisation. Interval coverage and power are not established.
There is no general model ranking or identified autonomous-play equilibrium;
draft text does not verify internal beliefs.

\section{Related work}

Akata et al.\ \cite{akata} study LLM behaviour in repeated games and a social
chain-of-thought intervention. Fontana et al.\ \cite{fontana} examine cooperative
behaviour against adversaries of varying hostility. Pal et al.\ \cite{pal}
examine strategies, continuation probabilities, horizons and framing in repeated
Prisoner's Dilemma. Our narrower extension compares bounded Direct/Draft
procedures in two hosted Nemotron deployments using exact committed-opponent
loss, alongside autonomous pairing outcomes. These results do not establish
superiority over those studies' models, tasks or methods.

in approximately 3 hours 8 minutes. Estimated final inference cost was \$4.9278,

Drafting uses two requests per decision and longer inputs and outputs.
Table~\ref{tab:costs} includes both benchmark and autonomous play, so it is not
a Track A cost-effectiveness estimate. Public actions cannot independently
validate usage aggregates or provider billing. The completed run used local
CPU coordination and remote Token Factory inference; earlier cloud job probes
failed before scientific inference.

The public app offers an action-by-action study replay and aggregate results.
Its live educational mode instead uses temperature zero, reasoning requested off,
and optional reported forecasts; it does not rerun this scientific protocol.
